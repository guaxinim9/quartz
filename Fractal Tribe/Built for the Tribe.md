We are built for the tribe, yes? We evolved this way. So, when we do not live tribally, there is a disconnect. How many of the eternal ills of civilization can be explained as symptoms of this disconnect? That is, people go a little crazy when they lack a tribe. We know they get *really* crazy in solitary confinement, for the same reason: we need each other, we evolved to live together, we are nothing alone. So, surely a *deficit* of belonging and connection will make them act at least a little bit crazy, and sometimes really crazy like Vlad the Impaler or Qin Shi Huang or Jeffrey Dahmer—depending on the extent of the deficit. It's like with any need: unmet, it will kill you; met insufficiently, it will damn sure affect you. 

I came upon this way of thinking scientifically, through the doctrine of evolutionary psychology. That is, we evolved to live in tribes; we are still optimized for our evolutionary conditions. 

But religion reckons the exact same. "It is not well for man to be alone." God made us to live together. The early Christians organized themselves into small communities. In fact religious movements have always gone along this way. We are used to the archetype of the cult, which—it is a shame—overshadows countless thriving ashrams and ecovillages (the latter perhaps do not seem religious at first blush, but they are almost always motivated by a mystic sense of oneness with nature, and this kind of psychedelic pantheism is a coherent metaphysics more than capable of giving meaning)

We are used to thinking of commune-building as the doomed enterprise of idealists, and to some extent this is true—most communes fail. But religious communes are an obvious and shining exception. They have always functioned and flourished, and continue to do so to this day. Surrendering one's will to Christ would seem an absurdly irrational move, but the proof is in the proverbial pudding. 

There is probably some difference in kind between us and the other animals, owing to our unique time-consciousness and our language and our iterative culture. Terence McKenna believed that some transcendental object was pulling our species from raw animal existence into history and from history into hyperspace, and [[Christianity]] seems to believe something similar. The theory of evolution seems to be sufficient to explain the whole unfolding of life on earth, save for two miracles: the emergence of the first life form, and us.

So it would be dishonest for me to say that human beings are simply animals and so we are programmed for an objective function, same as any animal, and that this function is the tribe, hunting and gathering, and civilization is a mistake and it only makes us miserable—because I no longer believe this. Something much weirder is going on here.

But this is the line of thinking which originally led me to explore [[The Tribe]] as Eden, as the utopia in our DNA which informs all. And though it is not the whole truth, there is much truth in it. 

We did live in tribes for millions of years. Probably since we diverged from chimps 6 million years ago our union has grown evermore perfect. Being small and flimsy, a naked ape's best shot for survival is the power of friendship. Tribes lacking the spirit of solidarity won't last. 

If you're an ape optimized for the jungle, you are not going down onto the savannah willingly. The idea is that tectonic shifts caused a change in the climate which eliminated the jungle which at the time existed in East Africa. While this was starting to happen, bigger and more dominant proto-chimps bullied our ancestors out of the trees. We went down to the savannah as a last resort.
